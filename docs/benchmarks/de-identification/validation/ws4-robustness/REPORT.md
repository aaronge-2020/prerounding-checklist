# Workstream 4 — Robustness & Generalizability

**Scope:** note-type generalizability (discharge summaries / nursing notes / progress
notes), a targeted edge-case suite, and a measurement-grounded limitations list for
the publication. This workstream does **not** duplicate accuracy-leaderboard work;
it asks whether the browser-local de-identification pipeline behaves consistently
when the input distribution shifts.

**Pipeline under test:** the shipped app code at `~/workspace/prerounding/repo`,
run verbatim in headless Chromium via the copied benchmark harness
(`harness/`, model `onnx-community/stanford-deidentifier-base-ONNX`, quantized,
hybrid mode = NER model + deterministic rules + temporal fallback + clinician
review gate). No pipeline code was modified or tuned for this workstream.

**Harness provenance:** `site/` is a verbatim copy of the benchmark site
tree (pipeline bundle, ONNX model, vendored transformers/ORT); `harness/driver.mjs`
and `harness/server.mjs` are copies with only path/port env overrides;
`scoring/<type>/harness/score.py` are byte-identical copies of the benchmark
scorer (md5 `0c8f0fb7488b9f63499daa91908b413f`).

## 1. Note-type generalizability

### Method
75 synthetic clinical notes (25 per type) were generated by `notes/gen_notes.py`
(seed 20260929) with character-offset PHI ground truth. **Every note, regardless
of type, carries an identical PHI quota**: 4 NAME, 3 DATE, 1 TIME, 1 PHONE,
1 DOB, 1 MRN (ID), 3 LOCATION, 1 ADDRESS, plus EMAIL in every 3rd note —
15–16 gold spans per note, 383 spans per set, identical category mix across all
three sets (verified by assertion in the analysis). Only the prose style differs:
discharge summaries are structured multi-section narratives (~94 words/note),
nursing notes are short fragmented timestamped entries (~73 words/note), progress
notes are SOAP-format (~79 words/note). All names, addresses, phones
(555-01xx), and dates are fictional.

Scoring: exact-span micro P/R/F1 (primary), per-category exact-span F1, plus the
scorer's secondary overlap and character-level metrics. Hybrid mode (the deployed
configuration) is primary.

### Results (hybrid mode, exact-span)

| Note type | n | Gold spans | Words/note | PHI/100w | P | R | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Discharge summaries | 25 | 383 | 93.9 | 16.31 | 0.7378 | 0.5509 | 0.6308 |
| Nursing notes | 25 | 383 | 73.3 | 20.89 | 0.7838 | 0.6815 | 0.7291 |
| Progress notes (SOAP) | 25 | 383 | 79.4 | 19.28 | 0.7565 | 0.6084 | 0.6744 |

Cross-type exact-span F1 spread: 0.098 (max nursing 0.7291 − min discharge 0.6308).

Secondary metrics (hybrid): discharge overlap F1 0.855 / char-level F1 0.9185;
nursing overlap F1 0.9274 / char-level F1 0.9011; progress overlap F1 0.8915 /
char-level F1 0.9422. Model-only (raw checkpoint as the app consumes it):
discharge F1 0.2993, nursing F1 0.4269, progress F1 0.2885.

### Verdict on cross-type degradation

**The pipeline does not degrade across note types on this synthetic
distribution — the hypothesized degradation is not observed.** Exact-span
hybrid F1 is highest on nursing notes (0.729), then progress notes (0.674),
then discharge summaries (0.631); cross-type spread is 0.098. Per-category F1
is nearly flat across types for NAME (0.50 everywhere — driven by span-boundary
conventions, see below), TIME/DOB/EMAIL (1.00 everywhere), and LOCATION (0.00
everywhere). The spread comes from *template-construction × rule* interactions,
not from the model struggling with fragmented text:

- DATE is 0.80 on discharge but 0.97–1.00 on nursing/progress because all 25
  discharge misses are the same construction: the follow-up date in
  "Follow up with Dr. {name} on {date} at {time}" (25/25 missed; header and
  course dates are all caught). A targeted probe showed the trigger is a date
  directly followed by "at {clock time}" — "Follow up on 08/15/2026 at 2:15 PM"
  drops the date while "See Dr. Priya Cohen on 08/15/2026" (no time) keeps it.
- ADDRESS is 0.00 on discharge/progress but 0.57 on nursing because the
  address rule merges "street, city, state" into one ADDRESS span
  ("9925 Eastern Ave, Annapolis, Maryland"), matching none of the four
  separate gold spans; nursing notes often have the street standing alone
  ("Home: 7594 Harford Rd."), which matches exactly 10/25 times.
- In model-only mode, NAME exact-span F1 is actually *best* on nursing notes
  (0.746 vs 0.500 discharge/progress): without the hybrid rules' span
  expansion (which folds in titles — "Dr. Rachel Fischer" — and even adjacent
  words — "Called Dr. Rachel Brooks", "Laura White    MRN"), the raw model
  output aligns with name-only gold spans more often.

Caveat: category mix was held artificially identical across types; in real EHRs
the mix differs by type, so real-world cross-type gaps could differ. And these
are the author's own synthetic templates — regular by construction (see Honesty
appendix).

## 2. Edge-case suite

27 targeted synthetic snippets (`notes/gen_edge.py`, seed 777; ground truth in
`data-edge/expected.json`). PASS = every expected span ≥80% covered by a hybrid
predicted entity; `expect_absent` spans must have zero overlap. Judged by
`notes/judge_edge.py` against `results-edge/raw_hybrid.json`.

**Pass rate: 20/27 (74%).**

| Case | Expectation | Result | One-line note |
|---|---|---|---|
| E01 | "Jonh Smtih" (misspelled name) | PASS | covered 100% |
| E02 | "JOHN SMITH" all-caps + DOB | PASS | name and DOB both covered |
| E03 | "jane o'brien" lowercase + apostrophe | PASS | covered 100% |
| E04 | "Mary-Jane Watson-Parker" hyphenated | PASS | covered 100% |
| E05 | "24th Sept 2026" ordinal date | PASS | temporal pass handles ordinals |
| E06 | "09.24.2026" dot-separated date | PASS | covered 100% |
| E07 | "Sept 24th" yearless date | PASS | flagged as DATE |
| E08 | "+1 (410) 555-0132" | PASS | covered 100% |
| E09 | "410.555.0132" dot phone | PASS | covered 100% |
| E10 | "+44 20 7946 0958" international | PASS | country-code format handled |
| E11 | "Test Patient" placeholder | **FAIL** | zero coverage — no entity of any label overlaps it |
| E12 | "John Doe" placeholder | PASS | caught (via alias machinery; labeled PROVIDER NAME) |
| E13 | "Patient X" | PASS | correctly not flagged (specificity holds) |
| E14 | "Smith,John" (no space after comma) | PASS | both parts covered |
| E15 | "DOB:\n01/02/1970" (line break) | PASS | date and MRN digits both caught |
| E16 | 2,325-word note, PHI at very end | PASS | all 4 expected found incl. name at final character; no truncation; 44.6 s |
| E17 | "Dr. Alan Smith" / "Smith" / "J. Smith" | PASS | all three alias variants covered |
| E18 | "47-year-old" | **FAIL** | no entity predicted at all |
| E19 | "age 47" | **FAIL** | no entity predicted at all |
| E20 | "47 y/o" | **FAIL** | no entity predicted at all |
| E21 | "92-year-old" and "age 91" | **FAIL** | no entity predicted at all (ages 90+ are explicitly HIPAA-sensitive) |
| E22 | "MRN# 0045123" | PASS | digits caught as ID/MRN |
| E23 | "chart 45123" bare number | PASS | caught as ID |
| E24 | "000-12-3456" SSN-format | PASS | caught as ID |
| E25 | "j.smith+clinic@examplemail.com" | PASS | plus-tag email caught |
| E26 | "1234 W Pratt St Apt 5B, Baltimore MD 21201" | **FAIL** | street match cut off before "5B" (trailing space kept); "Baltimore" missed entirely; "MD 21201" caught as LOCATION |
| E27 | "09/24/2026 at 2:30 PM" | **FAIL** | date dropped when directly followed by "at {clock time}"; the time is kept |

### The three most important failures

1. **Ages are completely undetected (E18–E21).** "47-year-old", "age 47",
   "47 y/o", "92-year-old", "age 91" — the pipeline emitted zero entities in
   all four snippets. Age is a HIPAA identifier, and ages 90+ require special
   handling; a note stating "the 92-year-old was admitted" would pass through
   with the age intact.
2. **"Test Patient" is invisible to the pipeline (E11).** The exact string
   produces no prediction of any label — 0% coverage, not even a partial flag
   on "Test" or "Patient". (In the same snippet the DOB *was* caught, so the
   failure is specific to the name.) "John Doe" *is* caught and "Patient X" is
   correctly left alone, so the gap is specific to this placeholder phrasing.
3. **A calendar date followed by "at {clock time}" loses the date (E27, plus
   25/25 discharge notes).** "Procedure scheduled 09/24/2026 at 2:30 PM" keeps
   the TIME but drops the DATE. A targeted probe (notes/probe_date.mjs)
   isolated the trigger: "Follow up on 08/15/2026 at 2:15 PM" → date missed;
   "The follow-up is on 08/15/2026" → date caught; "See Dr. Priya Cohen on
   08/15/2026" (provider adjacent, no time) → date caught. It is the
   date+time adjacency, not the provider name, that drops the date.

### "Test Patient" verdict

**Confirmed: the known gap is real and total.** In "This is a note about Test
Patient, DOB 01/02/1970.", the pipeline flagged the DOB but produced no entity
overlapping "Test Patient" at all — 0% character coverage, no partial
prediction on either word, in hybrid mode. Any workflow relying on this
pipeline must treat "Test Patient" (and similar test-chart phrasings) as
unhandled: the clinician review gate is the only layer that can catch it.

### Long-note stress (E16)

2,325 words / 17,721 characters: completed with **no truncation** — entities
were detected at the final character ("Dr. Zachary Quill" [17703,17720]).
Latency was 44.6 s versus a 389 ms median for the short snippets (~115× slower
for ~100× the characters — roughly linear, but a long discharge summary costs
on the order of a minute on this machine under load).

## 3. Known-limitations list (for the paper)

Each bullet is grounded in a measurement from this workstream — no speculation
beyond the data.

1. **Ages are not detected at all.** "47-year-old", "age 47", "47 y/o",
   "92-year-old", "age 91" produced zero predicted entities in every probe
   (edge cases E18–E21). Age is a HIPAA identifier, and ages over 89 require
   special handling; a note reading "the 92-year-old was admitted" passes
   through with the age intact.
2. **"Test Patient" is invisible to the pipeline.** The exact placeholder
   string yields no prediction of any label — 0% character coverage, not even
   a partial flag (edge case E11). "John Doe" is caught and "Patient X" is
   correctly ignored, so the gap is specific to this phrasing; the clinician
   review gate is the only layer that can catch it.
3. **A calendar date directly followed by "at {clock time}" loses the date.**
   "Procedure scheduled 09/24/2026 at 2:30 PM" keeps the TIME but drops the
   DATE (E27); the same construction accounts for all 25 missed DATEs in the
   discharge set ("Follow up with Dr. {name} on {date} at {time}", DATE recall
   0.667 vs 0.97–1.00 on nursing/progress). A probe isolated the trigger to
   the date+time adjacency itself, not the preceding provider name.
4. **City and full state names without a street address are never detected as
   locations.** 0 of 225 gold LOCATION spans were flagged across all 75 notes
   (LOCATION F1 0.00 in every note type). "Lives in Baltimore, Maryland."
   yields nothing; "Annapolis, Maryland" is labeled FACILITY by the model.
   Only the "{2-letter state} {ZIP}" shape ("MD 21201") is caught as LOCATION
   by rule.
5. **The model mislabels ZIP codes as DATE or ID.** In nursing notes, every
   standalone 5-digit ZIP was flagged by the model with the wrong label — 5 as
   DATE, 7 as ID (all 12 prediction errors in those categories). A ZIP is
   therefore either missed, mislabeled, or swallowed into an ADDRESS span, but
   essentially never correctly typed as a location.
6. **Name spans are systematically over-extended, depressing exact-span NAME
   scores.** Hybrid NAME exact-span F1 is 0.50 in all three note types because
   the rules fold titles and adjacent words into the span ("Dr. Rachel
   Fischer", "Called Dr. Rachel Brooks", "Laura White    MRN" — the latter
   swallowing the following "MRN" label word). The names *are* covered
   (overlap F1 0.86–0.93), but boundaries don't match a name-only gold
   standard; the raw model output aligns better (nursing NAME F1 0.75
   model-only vs 0.50 hybrid).
7. **The NER checkpoint contributes almost nothing for structured categories.**
   Model-only DATE, PHONE, EMAIL, TIME, DOB, LOCATION, and ADDRESS F1 are all
   0.00 on discharge and progress notes; hybrid-mode detection of these
   categories comes entirely from deterministic rules and the temporal pass.
   The model carries NAME (partially) and MRN-like digit strings.
8. **Address detection depends on the street being followed by city/state.**
   When the street stands alone ("Home: 7594 Harford Rd."), it was caught
   10/25 times in nursing notes; with an apartment number ("1234 W Pratt St
   Apt 5B") the match cuts off before "5B". When city/state follow, street,
   city, and state merge into one ADDRESS span that matches none of the four
   separate gold spans (ADDRESS exact-span F1 0.00 on discharge/progress).
9. **Long notes are slow but not truncated.** A 2,325-word / 17,721-character
   note completed with entities detected at the final character, taking
   44.6 s versus a 389 ms median for short snippets on the test machine —
   roughly linear scaling, but a long discharge summary costs on the order of
   a minute.
10. **All of the above was measured on synthetic notes from the author's own
    templates** (see Honesty appendix): regular phrasing, controlled PHI
    placement. Real EHR text — with typos, copy-paste artifacts, and denser
    PHI — will differ; these numbers are a consistency check, not a
    real-world performance estimate.

## Honesty appendix: what synthetic testing can and cannot show

- These notes come from the author's own templates, not from real clinical text:
  vocabulary, sentence structure, and PHI placement are far more regular than in
  real EHR notes. Measured F1 on this distribution is an upper-bound-style
  sanity check, not an estimate of real-world performance.
- Category mix was held artificially identical across note types; in real EHRs
  the mix differs by type (e.g., nursing notes are time-dense), so real
  cross-type gaps may be larger or smaller.
- The edge cases probe single phenomena in isolation; real notes combine them.
- No tuning was performed against these sets; the pipeline saw them for the
  first time at measurement.
